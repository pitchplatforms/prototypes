01

What a note is

The whole plan rests on one structural claim. If you disagree with it, disagree now — everything downstream assumes it.

A business borrows money from the crowd instead of selling shares. Investors lend in small fractions of one loan. The rate is fixed, the repayment schedule is fixed and published up front, and money comes back on a clock — typically monthly — until the loan is repaid. Nobody owns any part of the business, and nobody is waiting for an exit.

That is the product difference. The engineering claim is narrower and more useful:

A note is a campaign. Same countdown, same close, same rails — with debt terms attached, and a repayment schedule that starts after it closes.

So we are not building a second platform. We are teaching the platform we already run a second kind of raise. No parallel journey, no separate app — the same reuse philosophy the team already ratified for other products.

Who borrows
A vetted business — an issuer. We underwrite it and give it a credit grade before it can list.
Who lends
Investors, in fractions. Each one holds a note — their slice of the loan, with their own document.
What they get
Their money back plus interest, on a published schedule. Not shares, not upside — a repayment promise.
At launch
All four products — receivables, payables, term and micro. Conventional only; Islamic notes are deliberately out.
Out of scope
Secondary market. A note is held to maturity at MVP.
02

What changes about what we already have

A full inventory of all four codebases found exactly one P2P artifact in the entire stack. It is a domain greenfield — but the chassis underneath turned out to be unusually reusable.

70/30

Roughly 70% of the P2P journey rides rails we already have. The genuinely new builds are the servicing engine, e-mandate collection, credit scoring and the default lifecycle.

Reuse as-is
Notes just use it. No change.
  • Campaign lifecycleCountdown, cooling, allotment, success or fail — the same state machine. A note succeeds at 100% of its minimum target
  • KYC and investor categoriesAn investor who can already do ECF can invest in a note
  • Consent captureHow we prove who agreed to what, and to which version of it
  • Document generationPDFs, monthly statements, tax certificates — new templates only
  • Scheduled jobsThe machinery that runs things on a clock, already mature
  • Email and in-app noticesExisting channels, new message types
Extend
Exists, learns one new trick.
  • The apply journeyToday it splits by date. It has to split by what you are raising.
  • Invest and paySame pledge, pay, refund pipeline — plus three acknowledgements, and it mints the investor's note document on payment
  • Investor limitsNotes get their own cap — RM50,000 retail, measured on outstanding P2P exposure. Not pooled with ECF or PSTX.
  • The walletPay in by wallet or by FPX and DuitNow. Every payout lands in the wallet — no exceptions.
  • The listing pageOne consolidated browse across every instrument, with the type shown as a tag rather than a separate page.
  • Admin workspacePlus a credit file with a grade, and disbursement ops
Build new
Nothing like it exists anywhere.
  • Repayment schedule engineAmortising and bullet. The platform has never owed anyone a schedule.
  • Collection by e-mandateAuto-debit from the issuer's bank account. Needs a vendor.
  • Payout distributionSplitting one payment pro-rata across every holder. ECF dividends will use this too.
  • Credit scoring and gradingNo grading logic exists today. The grade is a mandatory public disclosure.
  • Arrears and days-past-dueTracking, late charges, escalation
  • Default lifecycleSeven states, shown to investors in real time
  • Issuer onboarding portalReplaces document collection over Google Drive and email
  • WhatsApp channelNo SMS or WhatsApp capability exists in the platform at all

The honest version: we have taken money in for years. We have never had to send money back out on a schedule. Repayment, distribution and default are the real new thing — and they are conveniently the parts a note's own clock lets us ship after launch day.

The gates

There are no dates in this plan. The sequence is not a schedule we chose — each gate is forced by the instrument itself, and skipping one is not an option available to us.

This is why the plan is ordered the way it is. Servicing and default work do not need to be finished for launch day — they need to be finished before the calendar reaches them, and the note itself tells us when that is.

The seven lanes

Cut along the path money takes through the product, so each owner holds one coherent story end to end. Click any lane for its scope, characteristics and how it lands on the code we already have.

Already done in ClickUp. Each lane is now its own Epic in the P2P Launch list — P2P-61 to P2P-67 — with its features moved underneath. P2P-25 is no longer the container; it is the go-live milestone. Every ticket kept its status, their dependencies and their feature branches. The Feature stays the unit that gets a branch and gets promoted (ADR-0017) — lane Epics are an organising layer and never a PR title. Two of the seven build capability the rest of the business inherits: the onboarding portal, which ECF migrates onto, and the payout engine, which ECF dividends will run on.

06

What could go wrong

Stated plainly, with what we intend to do about each. Add to this list in the session — that is partly what it is for.

The invest flow lives inside our most sensitive money code — two files of roughly 18,000 lines, with live ECF and PSTX running through them.

MitigationNew P2P logic goes in separate services that the existing code calls into, rather than surgery inside them. Proof against a real database, not mocked tests.

The wallet work this depends on is not merged and is gated on compliance sign-off.

MitigationPay-in could launch on direct rails alone if it slips. Payouts cannot — that one has no workaround and needs escalating early.

Distribution arithmetic has regulatory consequences — how a partial payment is split, how late charges accrue, rounding across hundreds of investors.

MitigationWorked examples written as tests before the code exists. Dry Run B has to reconcile to the ringgit.

E-mandate vendor procurement could outrun the engineering.

MitigationManual repayment recording is designed in from day one, not bolted on later.

The onboarding portal is a flagship UX project embedded inside a compliance launch. Scope creep is the default outcome.

MitigationIts must-have slice is the checklist, the per-document states and the reject loop. Everything else is explicitly an enhancement.

Our frontend tests prove a screen compiles, not that it renders. A blank page passes the build.

MitigationEvery new P2P screen ships with a render smoke test, and the implementer does not get to edit the test that grades their own screen.

ClickUp Epic P2P-61

Pure platform plumbing. No user-visible feature ships from this lane alone — and every other lane is blocked until it lands.

Key characteristics

  • Nothing here is architecturally novel. The risk is getting the data shape right, because all six other lanes inherit it.
  • Engineering-led. There is very little product judgement in it, which is why it can start immediately.
  • The config work carries a versioned change audit trail — several of these parameters are visible to the regulator, so who changed a number and when is part of the feature.

What it covers

How it lands on existing code

Extends

Note terms hang off a campaign as a companion record — the same satellite pattern the codebase already uses for campaign detail. No new entity family.

Reuses

New consent types plug straight into the existing versioned consent store. The PSTX risk disclosure already set the precedent for exactly this.

Reuses

The feature-flag pattern exists and is proven. Hiding the whole product line behind one switch is not new work.

New

The parameters engine with an audit trail. Config exists today but not in an admin-editable, change-tracked form.

Ops can start now, with zero engineering: the P2P application form template is already authorable in admin form management today.

ClickUp Epic P2P-62

A business applies, we underwrite it, we grade it, we admit it and set up its note. Mostly admin-side, and it contains the only real domain arithmetic in the build that is not money movement.

Key characteristics

  • The gate this lane serves is "first issuer onboarded". We cannot list a note we have not underwritten, so nothing in the investor lane matters until this works.
  • The credit decision itself stays human, permanently, by rulebook design. We are building the worksheet, not the decision-maker.
  • The scoring computes in admin from day one — chosen deliberately over results-entry, because it removes transcription risk and lets the investment committee recalibrate without a deployment.
  • The credit grade is a mandatory public disclosure. Whatever this lane produces ends up on the note's public page.

What it covers

F1.3 · P2P-32Credit assessment moduleA credit file per application: scoring worksheet, grade, rate, rationale, two-person sign-off, full audit historyM–L
F1.4 · P2P-33Note campaign setup & admissionDebt terms, schedule builder, disclosure assembly, admission checklist, and the issuer agreement signed by click-throughL
P2P-60Scoring manual v1The seed scorecard, bench-tested, plus the map of what data we must capture to calibrate it later—
P2P-59Committee monitoring packThe monthly scoring and performance extract the investment committee reviews—

How it lands on existing code

Reuses

The application step engine already does per-step approve and reject. P2P is another journey through machinery that works.

Extends

The newer application journey exists but branches by date, not by product. Re-keying it to branch on what you are raising is the real engineering in F1.1.

Reuses

Company credit reports are already fetched and attached at application time. They become the input to the scoring worksheet — no new integration.

Reuses

Two-person sign-off borrows the existing compliance dual-control pattern; the audit history borrows the shape of the risk-class history table.

New

No grading logic exists anywhere in the platform. The worksheet, the grade matrix and the rate derivation are all built from scratch.

Stays manual, by design: analyst due diligence, fit-and-proper checks, screening, and every committee decision. The rulebook requires a human on each one.

ClickUp Epic P2P-63

One feature, and the largest single UX investment in the programme. It replaces document collection over Google Drive and email with a checklist portal where the issuer can see exactly what is outstanding.

Key characteristics

  • It debuts on P2P and ECF migrates onto it afterwards. Because it is built on the existing engine, that migration is authoring a checklist template — not new engineering.
  • Research-backed: comparable lenders cut document turnaround from 42 days to 15 with this model, and 93% of what lenders ask for was already supplied earlier — so the checklist must never re-ask what the application already captured.
  • No Malaysian P2P competitor visibly offers per-document two-way review. This is a differentiator, not catch-up.
  • Highest scope-creep risk in the build. The must-have slice is small and named below; everything else is explicitly an enhancement.

What it covers

F1.2 · P2P-31Issuer onboarding portalAn item library defined once, named templates assembled from it, and an application snapshot frozen when an issuer starts — so a live application never shifts underneath them. Eight item types cover every way a requirement can be satisfied: file, form, acknowledgement, signature, payment, ops attestation, a read-only mirror of something we already know, and a task on another screen. Ops can return an item with the content preserved, waive it with a reason, or upload on the issuer's behalf. Reminders and OCR are out of launch scope.L–XL

How it lands on existing code

Reuses

Built on the existing step engine and dynamic forms — the same machinery the application journey runs on. This is the single decision that makes ECF migration cheap later.

Reuses

File upload, storage and attachment plumbing all exist. The portal orchestrates them; it does not reinvent them.

New

Templates and the snapshot — today a step row is global and live, so editing one changes every application in flight. Plus the return-with-content-preserved loop and per-item comment threads. Today this whole stretch is Drive and email — one-way, with no visibility for the issuer.

The discipline that keeps this from running away: a closed set of eight item types, an open catalogue of items. Ops can add, reorder and re-section items freely and pick a type; ops cannot invent a type — that is an engineering ticket. There is a clickable prototype covering all three sides of it: the issuer checklist, the ops review console, and the admin item library and template builder.

ClickUp Epic P2P-64

Everything an investor sees: finding a note, reading it, investing in it, holding it. The widest lane at seven features, the most public, and the strictest — almost nothing here is allowed to be manual.

Key characteristics

  • This lane is the public launch gate. Everything else can be staged; this cannot.
  • The investor executes their note by accepting terms at the moment they invest — a click-through, recorded as a versioned consent. No signing ceremony, no e-signature vendor at MVP.
  • Three acknowledgements are mandatory and must be captured as first-class records, not checkboxes we forget about.
  • The note's public page is a regulated disclosure document, not a marketing page — terms, issuer financials, the scoring explanation, the binding repayment schedule and default provisions all have to be on it.
  • Contains the sharpest technical risk in the build: the invest flow lands inside the platform's largest and most sensitive money code.

What it covers

F2.2 · P2P-35The note one-pagerThe full regulated disclosure set, plus the existing Q&A and updates tabsL
F2.3 · P2P-36Invest flow & note executionThree acknowledgements, funding by wallet or FPX and DuitNow, and the investor's own note document generated and filed on paymentL–XL
F2.4 · P2P-37Investor limitsNotes join the existing per-category totals; caps driven by configS–M
F2.5 · P2P-38Refunds & cancellationDuring the offer period only — a note is binding once executed, so there is no post-close cooling periodS–M
F2.6 · P2P-39Investor notes dashboardHoldings, per-note detail with schedule and status, document vaultL
F2.7 · P2P-40Disclosure & landing pagesScoring methodology explainer, the default-management policy in full, the public statistics page, and the P2P landing pageS–M

How it lands on existing code

Reuses

The invest flow already has the click-through mechanism we need — inline terms with checkboxes. It is currently ad hoc, and in one case hardcoded to a specific campaign; P2P makes it a first-class, versioned thing.

Extends

Pledge, pay and refund all exist, including how we record which rail the money came in on. Notes add acknowledgements and document generation to the same pipeline.

Extends

The listing page already carries more than one card type — the Token product proved it. F2.1 turns it into one consolidated browse across every instrument, with the type shown as a tag.

Extends

The limit checker exists and is config-driven. Notes get their own cap rather than joining the existing sum — RM50,000 retail on outstanding P2P exposure. It becomes a sum that already works.

New

The customer app has zero product branching today, and despite admin-side groundwork, no one-pager redesign exists in it. Sizing assumes we are building that from scratch.

Watch this one: F2.3 lands inside the platform's largest money service. New P2P logic goes in a separate service the existing code calls into, rather than surgery inside it — and it gets verified against a real database, not mocked tests.

ClickUp Epic P2P-65

Everything on the issuer's side of the money: closing the raise, paying them, then collecting each repayment when it falls due. Split from the payout lane deliberately — different counterparty, different failure modes.

Key characteristics

  • The first half serves "first campaign closes"; the second half serves "first repayment due". Both gates are set by the note's own clock, not by us.
  • Paying the issuer runs on a deliberately manual rail at launch — instructions are generated by the platform, then signed and sent by a human. The rulebook explicitly permits this until the bank integration lands.
  • Collection needs an e-mandate vendor we have not chosen yet. Manual repayment recording is designed in from day one so the code is never blocked on procurement.
  • Every payment out to the issuer is gated: the raise hit 80%, conditions precedent are cleared, and the final agreement is executed. Fees are netted at that moment.

What it covers

F3.2 · P2P-42Final agreement & issuer executionRegenerated with the real numbers, signed by click-through, and it gates disbursementM
F3.3 · P2P-43Disbursement opsFee-netting calculation, trustee instruction generation, two-person approval queueM
F3.4 · P2P-44Fees engineApplication, KYB, success fees and taxM
F4.1 · P2P-45Repayment schedule engineAmortising and bullet, and amendable when a note is restructuredM–L
F4.2 · P2P-46Collection & e-mandateEnrolment tracked from onboarding, execution through a provider, manual recording as fallbackL
F4.4 · P2P-48Reminders & arrears startPre-due reminders by email and WhatsApp, the first missed-payment alert, late-charge accrualM

How it lands on existing code

Reuses

The 80% success threshold and the whole close sequence already work for equity raises. F3.1 configures rather than rebuilds.

Reuses

The existing payment-recording modal in admin becomes the interim repayment tool from day one — that is the manual fallback, and it already exists.

Reuses

Scheduled-job machinery is mature and already runs campaign lifecycle and monthly crons. The repayment clock rides those rails.

Extends

Two-person approval reuses the pattern already proven on the wallet transaction queue.

New

No disbursement, schedule, or collection code exists anywhere in the platform. We have never paid an issuer after a close, and we have never asked anyone for money back.

Stays manual, by design: trustee instructions are signed by a named officer and the bank transfer is made by a human, until the bank integration lands. Suspense-account review stays with compliance.

ClickUp Epic P2P-66

One payment arrives from an issuer. It has to be split correctly across every holder, net of fees and tax, and land in their wallets. Built as a generic payout engine, not a P2P feature — interest for notes today, ECF dividends later.

Key characteristics

  • The highest-consequence arithmetic in the programme. Pro-rata splits, rounding across hundreds of investors, fee deduction, tax — all with regulatory consequences if wrong.
  • When a payment is short, it is allocated in a fixed order: recovery costs, then platform fees, then principal, then interest. That order is not ours to choose.
  • Everything lands in the pitchIN wallet. There is no alternative destination — which is why the unmerged wallet work is the single hardest external dependency in the plan.
  • Designing this as a general "split a pot across holders" capability rather than a note-specific one is a deliberate choice, and it is what makes ECF dividend payment mostly a configuration exercise later instead of a second build.

What it covers

F4.3 · P2P-47Matching, allocation & distributionCheck what arrived against what was owed, allocate it in the required order, split pro-rata across holders, deduct fees and tax, credit every wallet, issue receipts, update each instalment's statusXL
F4.5 · P2P-49Withdrawal limits & batch operationsSurfacing limits to investors taking money out, and the operational tooling to process them in bulkS–M
F4.6 · P2P-50Statements & tax certificatesExtending the existing generators to cover note incomeM

How it lands on existing code

Reuses

Monthly statement and tax-certificate generators already exist and are proven. F4.6 is new templates on working machinery.

Extends

The wallet, its holds, and the withdrawal approval queue all exist. This lane becomes a new source of credits into them.

New

There is no payout machinery in the platform today. The only dividend code that exists is a gain-and-loss calculator in the PSTX portfolio — it can account for a dividend event, but it has no way to actually pay one, and its own code comments say those events are not wired to anything.

New

Matching received money against a schedule, the allocation order, and pro-rata distribution net of fees — none of it has a precedent anywhere in the codebase.

How we keep it honest: worked examples written as tests before the code exists, and Dry Run B has to reconcile to the ringgit on a compressed clock before a real repayment is ever collected.

ClickUp Epic P2P-67

What happens when someone does not pay, what we are obliged to publish about it, and the two dry runs that gate go-live. Largely specified for us by the rulebook — less product judgement, more precise implementation.

Key characteristics

  • Last to build, and that is safe: the first note cannot reach default for 60 to 90 days after a missed payment. The runway is real.
  • A note's status is shown to investors in real time — seven states, publicly visible. This is not an internal admin view.
  • We are obliged to publish default statistics monthly. At launch the page truthfully shows a zero book with the methodology locked.
  • Contains both dry runs. Dry Run A gates public launch; Dry Run B gates the first real repayment cycle. Neither is optional.
  • Early warning at launch is deliberately light: a monthly bureau pull, human triage, and a hard block on further fundraising. Continuous monitoring comes later.

How it lands on existing code

Reuses

Notification rails, email and the announcement engines all exist. Notices are new templates and new triggers on working infrastructure.

Extends

The monthly regulatory report already exists for other products; P2P is an extension of it, not a new report.

New

Nothing arrears-shaped exists in the platform. No days-past-due, no late charges, no default states, no restructuring — none of these concepts appear anywhere in the code today.

New

The public statistics job. The obligation to publish is strict, but the formulas behind it are internal — so we control the arithmetic.

Stays manual, by design: collections casework, restructuring negotiation, recovery agents and legal action, write-off memos, and the watchlist meetings. All of it runs on ClickUp and ops, and the rulebook sanctions that.
