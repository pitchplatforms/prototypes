Guided walkthrough

The life of a tokenised fund,
from first filing to final buyback

7 chapters ≈ 8 minutes All figures in USD

Seven short videos cover the full life of a tokenised fund on the platform: intake, investor onboarding, the raise, allotment and token minting, reporting and voting, secondary trading, and redemption. Every screen is the working product.

One system carries the fund through every stage, so the register, custody records and trading history never need reconciling across providers.

The platform is a licensed operator of regulated investment crowdfunding and secondary trading, supervised by its home securities regulator.

Chapter 1 · 1:09

A regulated intake for funds

Every fund is vetted before it can raise. Your team reviews the entity, the documents, the offer terms and the campaign, and approves each item before anything goes live.

  • →Controlled listing. Four approvals stand between an application and a live campaign.
  • →Fund-native offers. Priced per unit against minimum and maximum targets; no valuation needed.
  • →Structured campaigns. A template covering mandate, fees, term and governance.
Chapter 2 · 0:44

Investor onboarding and custody

Investor protection is built into onboarding. One account covers subscribing, trading and custody.

  • →Identity first. No one invests before verification.
  • →Limits enforced. Three investor classes with hard annual caps, checked automatically.
  • →Custody included. Every investor gets a custodial wallet; no seed phrases, no network fees.
Chapter 3 · 1:18

A campaign page built for funds

Fund offers don't fit a startup crowdfunding template, so the campaign page is built around fund terms. The offer documents and the manager's answers sit on the page itself.

  • →Clear pricing. $1.00 per unit, 1,000 units per lot, minimum five lots.
  • →Full disclosure. Memorandum, partnership agreement, fee schedule and risk disclosure with the offer.
  • →Public Q&A. The manager answers investors on the record.
Chapter 4 · 0:56

Allotment and token minting

When a raise completes, the investor book becomes the register, and tokens are minted against it, one per unit. Nothing is carried over by hand.

  • →Authoritative register. 500,000 units and 38 holders, written at close.
  • →Digital certificates. Issued to every holder and regenerable from the back office at any time.
  • →Exact tokenisation. 10,000 units on the register means 10,000 tokens in the wallet.

The same lifecycle, shown on two funds

The walkthrough uses two funds because the later chapters need history. Nusantara Micro Fund I is created during the walkthrough. Meridian Logistics Fund I closed in December 2025 at $800,000, so it has an operating history. Both run on the same intake, register and custody.

Chapter 5 · 1:06

Reporting, NAV and investor voting

Funds stay accountable after the money is raised. Reporting and voting run on the same page investors subscribed on.

  • →Published NAV. Quarterly, to every holder; $1.42 per unit at 30 June 2026.
  • →On-platform voting. The register is the voter roll, and confirmed votes are final.
  • →Voting with full information. Each holder sees their own position and payout before voting.
Chapter 6 · 1:33

A regulated secondary market

Fund units are normally locked in for the term. The platform's secondary market lets investors sell without waiting it out.

  • →Regulated trading. Listed tickers, a live order book, and limit orders with full costs shown.
  • →Same-day settlement. A 3,000-unit trade at $1.35 settles and updates the register on trade date.
  • →No setup for buyers. Custody follows the trade automatically.
Chapter 7 · 0:49

Redemption by tender

Investors get a clean exit at fair value. The fund buys units back, pays cash to investor accounts, and the register and token supply are reduced together.

  • →Fair exit price. The buyback pays full NAV, about 5% above the market price.
  • →Optional participation. Holders who decline keep their units.
  • →Register and supply reduced together. Meridian's register falls from 800,000 to 488,000 units, and the bought-back tokens are burned to match.
