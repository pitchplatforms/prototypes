A regulated intake for funds

Nothing lists until it clears your review. The intake gives funds their own application track: entity, documents, offer terms, campaign story. Every item carries an approval your team signed.

  • →Four approval gates stand between an application and a live campaign.
  • →Fund offers are priced per unit against a target range, with no equity split to negotiate.
  • →A campaign template written for funds: mandate, fees, term, governance.
Chapter 2 · 0:44

Investor onboarding and custody

One onboarding sets an investor up for everything: subscribing, trading, custody. Identity checks gate the account, and classification caps what each tier can invest, enforced by the platform rather than by policy.

  • →Annual caps by investor class, checked at every subscription.
  • →A custodial wallet from day one: cash and every asset class in one balance.
  • →Investors hold tokens with nothing to configure and no keys to lose.
Chapter 3 · 1:18

A campaign page that speaks fund

The campaign page sells a fund in fund language: units and lots instead of equity, with the manager's answers published beside the offer for the life of the fund.

  • →$1.00 a unit, 1,000 units a lot, minimum five lots — and no valuation anywhere.
  • →Four documents in the offer pack: memorandum, agreement, fees, risks.
  • →Subscription to dashboard in one flow: ten lots, $10,000, declarations captured on the way.
Chapter 4 · 0:56

Allotment: one register, tokens to match

When a raise closes, the investor book becomes a register you operate — and tokens follow it one for one. Certificates, ownership types and quantities live on the same record the tokens mirror.

  • →Closed at $500,000 · 38 investors · 500,000 units allotted.
  • →A digital certificate for every holder, regenerable from the back office.
  • →10,000 units on the register, 10,000 tokens in the wallet. Always equal.

The same lifecycle, shown on two funds

Two funds carry the walkthrough because the later chapters need history. Nusantara Micro Fund I is built from a blank application; Meridian Logistics Fund I — closed December 2025 at $800,000 — is eight months in, old enough to report, trade and redeem. Same intake, same register, same custody.

Chapter 5 · 1:06

Reporting, NAV and investor voting

The fund keeps reporting where investors already are. NAV, quarterly updates and corporate votes run on the campaign page itself, and the register decides who votes.

  • →A published NAV series, quarter by quarter: $1.42 a unit at 30 June 2026.
  • →Holders who haven't voted are prompted at every sign-in; confirmation makes the vote final.
  • →Each holder votes on their own numbers: units, payout, return.
Chapter 6 · 1:33

A regulated secondary market

Fund units are normally locked up for the term. This platform lets your clients trade theirs: a listed ticker, a live order book, and fills that reach the register the same day.

  • →Limit orders with the full cost shown before confirmation.
  • →A single trade rewrites the register: seller to 7,000 units, a buyer added at 3,000.
  • →Custody follows on its own; buyers need no wallet setup.
Chapter 7 · 0:49

Redemption by tender

Closed-end funds still need an exit. The platform runs it as a tender: the fund buys back at NAV, cash settles to accounts, tokens burn, and the register comes down with the supply.

  • →$1.42 at full NAV against a market about 5% below — the reason holders tender.
  • →Mint on allotment, burn on redemption; the register authoritative at both ends.
  • →Holders who decline keep their units — participation is recorded per holder.
