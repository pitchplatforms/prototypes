The life of a tokenised fund,
from first filing to final buyback

7 chapters ≈ 8 minutes All figures in USD

Seven short videos cover the full life of a tokenised fund on the platform: intake, investor onboarding, the raise, allotment and token minting, reporting and voting, secondary trading, and redemption. Every screen is the working product.

The platform covers the whole lifecycle on one system: fundraising, registry, custody, reporting, trading and redemption.

The platform is a licensed operator of regulated investment crowdfunding and secondary trading, supervised by its home securities regulator.

Chapter 1 · 1:09

A regulated intake for funds

Funds apply through a dedicated intake. Your team reviews the entity, its documents, the offer terms and the campaign, and approves each item before anything goes live.

  • →Four approval gates between application and live campaign.
  • →Offers are priced per unit against minimum and maximum targets.
  • →A campaign template built for funds: mandate, fees, term, governance.
Chapter 2 · 0:44

Investor onboarding and custody

A single onboarding covers subscribing, trading and custody. Identity verification is required before investing, and each investor class has annual limits that the platform enforces automatically.

  • →Three investor classes with hard annual limits.
  • →A custodial wallet for every investor, opened automatically.
  • →No seed phrases or network fees for investors.
Chapter 3 · 1:18

A campaign page that speaks fund

Fund campaigns use fund terms: units and lots, a minimum subscription, and no valuation. The offer documents and the manager's Q&A sit on the campaign page.

  • →$1.00 per unit, 1,000 units per lot, minimum five lots.
  • →The offer pack: information memorandum, partnership agreement, fee schedule, risk disclosure.
  • →Subscriptions include the required declarations and appear on the investor's dashboard.
Chapter 4 · 0:56

Allotment: one register, tokens to match

When a raise closes, the investor book is written into the platform's register and tokens are minted against it, one per unit.

  • →Closed at $500,000 with 38 investors; 500,000 units allotted.
  • →Each holder has a digital certificate, managed from the back office.
  • →Register and wallet always match: 10,000 units, 10,000 tokens.

The same lifecycle, shown on two funds

The walkthrough uses two funds because the later chapters need history. Nusantara Micro Fund I is created during the walkthrough. Meridian Logistics Fund I closed in December 2025 at $800,000 and is eight months into its life. Both run on the same intake, register and custody.

Chapter 5 · 1:06

Reporting, NAV and investor voting

Reporting stays on the campaign page investors subscribed on. The fund publishes NAV and quarterly updates, and corporate actions are voted on-platform with the register as the voter roll.

  • →NAV published quarterly: $1.42 per unit at 30 June 2026.
  • →Holders are reminded to vote at each sign-in; confirmed votes are final.
  • →Each holder sees their own position and payout before voting.
Chapter 6 · 1:33

A regulated secondary market

Fund units are normally illiquid. On the platform they trade on a regulated secondary market, and every fill updates the register on trade date.

  • →Limit orders, with fees and totals shown before confirmation.
  • →The trade in the video moves 3,000 units; both holdings update the same day.
  • →Buyers need no wallet setup; custody is automatic.
Chapter 7 · 0:49

Redemption by tender

Redemption runs as a tender. The fund buys units back at NAV, cash is paid to investor accounts, tokens are burned and the register is reduced.

  • →$1.42 per unit at full NAV, against a market price about 5% lower.
  • →Tokens are minted at allotment and burned at redemption.
  • →Holders who decline keep their units.
