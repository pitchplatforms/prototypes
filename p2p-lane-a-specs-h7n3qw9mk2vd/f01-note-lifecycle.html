P2P-26 · the life of one note

One loan, three people, seven tables: what happens, and what we write down

A note is a loan from many investors to one company, repaid on a schedule. Follow one note from setup to the last repayment. Every step names who acts, what happens, which rows we record, and how the equity product does the same thing today.

Admin · pitchIN staff, and the steps the platform runs for them Investor · a person lending money Issuer · the company borrowing
The cast

Three people and seven tables

AdminpitchIN staff working in the admin portal, plus the automatic steps the platform runs on their behalf, such as starting the cooling-off period at the end date.
InvestorA person lending money through the pitchIN site. We follow three: Aisha, retail, RM 5,000. Ben, angel, RM 20,000, who changes his mind. Chong, sophisticated, RM 50,000.
IssuerContoh Kitchen Sdn Bhd, a food manufacturer borrowing RM 400,000 to 500,000 for 36 months at 10.5% a year, repaid monthly, in units of RM 100.
CampaignThe listing and the fundraise. Shared by every product. Gains three fields for notes.
CampaignNoteTermThe loan's terms as offered. One row per note campaign, frozen after setup.
InvestmentEach investor's payment and how many units it buys. Already exists; every product uses it.
NoteThe issued loan itself, created at close. Carries the terms in force and the servicing terms, the note's status and the running totals of what the issuer has repaid. Equity's equivalent is the share counter set up after allotment.
NoteRegisterWho holds what. One row per holder, created at close by adding up that holder's paid investments, with a holding status of its own and that holder's running share of every repayment. Equity's equivalent is the holder record on the counter.
RepaymentScheduleWhen money is due. Kept in versions so an old promise is never overwritten.
PayoutsMoney received and shared out to holders. Designed now, built with the repayment features.

"Every product" in this database means equity, in conventional and Islamic share classes, and now notes. Token campaigns run in a separate application today; they appear in the map at the end as future work.

Step by step

The story of N-2026-00001

Stage 1 · Set up
1

Admin approves the loan and sets up the note

Admin

Contoh Kitchen applied to borrow. Admin's credit team scored it B1, which fixes the rate band and the maximum tenure. Admin then creates the note in the admin portal: the platform works out the tenure unit and the default class from "monthly", checks the rate and tenure against the platform's limits, gives the note its number, sets the unit at RM 100, and copies the fee terms in force today onto the note so a later fee change cannot touch this deal.

We record
  • Campaign one new listing: product type Note, Conventional, target RM 400,000 to 500,000, minimum investment RM 100, price per asset RM 100 (the unit). Minimum investment and price per asset are decided when the note is set up, but they live on Campaign because every product has them: equity's price per share is this same column, renamed. How investments step up from the minimum is a separate ticket for every product. The equity-only columns on the same row (shares per lot, lots, shares issued, equity offered, share types, oversubscription) stay at their defaults. Nothing reads them for a note, and they move to their own equity table later.
  • CampaignNoteTerm one row: N-2026-00001, Business Term, 36 months, 10.50%, grade B1, purpose working capital, the reason for the grade, and the frozen fee terms (success fee 4%, late fee RM 500, 7 days grace, default at 90 days). Only debt-shaped facts live here. Frozen from here on.
  • RepaymentSchedule version 1, "Indicative", marked current: 36 monthly rows worked out on the RM 500,000 maximum, so investors can see the shape of repayments.
Equity today
Same for the shared part. Price per asset and minimum investment sit on the Campaign row for both products. Different for the rest: equity keeps its lot and share fields on Campaign today and moves them to their own table later; a note keeps its debt terms on their own table from day one.
ticket: F1.4 setup screen calls this ticket's note-terms service · limits and fee values from P2P-27 · schedule maths from F4.1
Stage 2 · Raise · the campaign is live
2

Aisha invests RM 5,000 and gets 50 units

Investor

Aisha reads the listing: rate, tenure, grade, the reason for the grade, and the indicative schedule. She confirms the three acknowledgements, that she understands the risks, that her investor category is still retail, and that she has read the disclosure. She agrees to the note's terms and, through the platform terms, authorises pitchIN to execute her note on her behalf at close, when the final amount and schedule are known. That is her binding act; the note document itself is produced at close. She pays RM 5,000 from her wallet. The platform checks the RM 50,000 retail cap, works out that RM 5,000 at RM 100 a unit is 50 units, and records it. Her money moves into the trust account and stays there until the campaign closes.

We record
  • Investment one row: Aisha, RM 5,000, retail, status Pending Payment, then Paid once the money lands. The same row says what the money buys, in instrument-agnostic columns: asset type Note, price per asset RM 100 copied from the campaign, asset quantity 50, held through the nominee (always true for notes), her account number. Lots are not used for notes.
  • UserConsent rows for the acknowledgements and the agreement to the terms, each with the version of the wording she accepted.
  • Note and NoteRegister nothing. Neither exists yet; she has a paid investment in an open campaign.
Equity today
Identical table, same statuses. For equity the same columns hold the share type, price per share and share quantity; the columns are renamed to say asset rather than share so both products read them the same way. One difference: equity also counts lots; notes never do.
ticket: lane D invest flow · consents P2P-28 · units = amount ÷ the campaign's price per asset · increments for every product are P2P-68, out of scope here · retail cap counts paid pledges in open note campaigns plus principal on live notes (P2P-27 screen 2, F2.4) · the authority to execute at close is worded in the platform terms, F-07, Legal to confirm
3

Ben invests RM 20,000, then changes his mind

Investor

While the campaign is still open, Ben asks for his money back. The rulebook allows this at any time before close. His RM 20,000 returns to his wallet.

We record
  • Investment Ben's row moves from Paid to Refunded, with the refunded amount. His 200 units are never issued.
  • NoteRegister nothing, now or ever. Ben never held a note, so the register never hears of him.
Equity today
Identical. A refund before close is the same Investment status change.
4

Chong and 187 others invest

Investor

Chong, a sophisticated investor with no cap, puts in RM 50,000 for 500 units. By the end date, 189 paid investments total RM 450,000, or 4,500 units. The platform stops accepting money at the RM 500,000 maximum, so a note is never oversubscribed. That rule belongs to the invest flow and still needs confirming there.

We record
  • Investment 189 rows with status Paid. The Campaign row shows the amount raised climbing.
Equity today
One difference. Equity can allow oversubscription and scales investors back at allotment. A note stops at its maximum, so there is nothing to scale back at close.
Stage 3 · Close
5

The campaign ends, cools off, and Admin decides

Admin

At the end date the platform moves the campaign into its cooling-off period, exactly as it does for equity; investors may still withdraw during it. When cooling-off ends, Admin reviews the raise and decides: RM 450,000 is above the RM 400,000 minimum and the conditions are met, so Admin confirms success. Had Admin failed it, every paid investment would be refunded and neither the note nor its register would ever exist.

We record
  • Campaign status Live → Cooling at the end date, then Success on Admin's decision, with the completion time. (Or Fail, and 189 Investment rows move to Refunded.)
Equity today
Identical. The same scheduler starts cooling-off, and a person completes or fails the campaign.
ticket: F3.1 close · success = 100% of the minimum target · the cooling-off length for notes is a P2P-27 parameter if it differs from equity
6

Contoh Kitchen signs the final terms

Issuer

The raise came in at RM 450,000, not the RM 500,000 the indicative plan assumed, so the platform produces the final schedule on the real amount, and the issuer digitally signs the Note Supplement that binds it. The indicative version is kept: investors were shown it.

We record
  • RepaymentSchedule version 2, "Final", becomes the current one: 36 rows on RM 450,000, first instalment due 1 November 2026. Version 1 stays on file, no longer current.
Equity today
No equivalent. Shares have no repayment plan. This table exists only for notes.
ticket: F3.1 close generates the version through this ticket's schedule versioning · Note Supplement document lane D
7

Admin mints the note and its register

Admin

This is the moment the loan comes into existence. The platform creates the note itself, RM 450,000 in 4,500 units, status Issued. Then it creates one register row per holder, adding up that holder's paid investments in the order the first was made, numbers it, generates and executes the holder's digital note under the authority given at payment, points each investment at its row and marks it Completed. Aisha holds slice 0001, 50 units; Chong holds slice 0003, 500 units; an investor who had paid twice would still hold one slice, exactly as equity merges a repeat investor into one Share. Ben's refunded investment is skipped. From here on, the note's status on the Note row describes the issuer's repayment of the one loan, and each register row carries its own holding status for what can happen to one holder alone: a buyback, later a transfer. Payouts go to Active holders only.

We record
  • Note one row: N-2026-00001, RM 450,000 issued, 4,500 units, status Issued, issued 1 October 2026, principal outstanding RM 450,000, nothing repaid yet, and the terms in force copied from the offer: 10.50%, 36 months, monthly, Class I, maturing 1 October 2029, together with the servicing terms the note discloses: grace days, the delinquency and default thresholds, the early-settlement rate and the late fees. This row carries everything servicing needs from now on; the offer on CampaignNoteTerm never changes and is never read again. Both schedule versions are stamped with the note's id, so servicing finds the plan by note too.
  • NoteRegister 189 rows, holding status Active. Aisha's: N-2026-00001-0001, 50 units, RM 5,000, linked from her investment (which records that she was retail and carries her consent from payment day), to Contoh Kitchen, and to her executed note document; principal outstanding RM 5,000, nothing received yet.
  • Investment 189 rows move from Paid to Completed.
Equity today
Same moment, same pattern. When Admin completes share allotment, equity marks the investments Completed, creates the Share rows, and sets up the company's share counter with its own status and one holder record per investor with its own status. The note and its register are that counter and those holder records. Two differences: equity merges an investor's several investments into one holder record, while each paid note investment is its own slice with its own document; and equity's allotment is drafted and reviewed by hand, while minting is one step once Admin has confirmed success.
ticket: this ticket's minting operation, called by F3.1 · note status values: Issued · Performing · Late · Restructured · Default in recovery · Default with low or no recovery · Written off · Closed (a raise found unsuccessful never gets a Note row; its refunds sit on the Investment rows) · holding status values: Active · Exited · Written off, with exit date and amount
Stage 4 · Disburse
8

Admin instructs the trustee; Contoh Kitchen receives the money

Admin

Admin instructs the independent trustee to release the proceeds: RM 450,000 less the 4% success fee plus tax, less stamp duty (nil until the end of 2026). The loan is now live and the repayment clock starts.

We record
  • Note status Issued → Performing, with the disbursement date and the maturity date. The 189 register rows do not change.
Equity today
No equivalent. Shares do not perform or fall behind. Completing an equity allotment opens the counter for secondary trading, which notes never do: a note cannot be sold before maturity.
ticket: F3.3 disbursement calls this ticket's disbursed transition · fees F3.4
Stage 5 · Repay
9

Contoh Kitchen pays the first instalment

Issuer

On 1 November 2026, RM 16,437.50 arrives in the trust account by direct debit: RM 12,500 of principal and RM 3,937.50 of interest, as the final schedule promised. The platform matches it to schedule row 1, splits it across the Active holders in proportion to their units, takes the 15% management fee on the interest plus 8% tax on that fee, and credits each wallet. Aisha, with 50 of 4,500 units, receives RM 175.55; Chong, with 500, receives RM 1,755.51.

We record
  • Payouts one event for the money received, pointing at schedule row 1, and 189 allocations: gross, fee, net, and the wallet credit for each holder. This is the ledger; everything below is a running total kept in step with it.
  • Note running totals: principal repaid RM 12,500, interest paid RM 3,937.50, principal outstanding RM 437,500, last paid 1 November 2026. Status still Performing.
  • NoteRegister each holder's running share. Aisha's row: principal repaid RM 138.89, interest received RM 43.75, principal outstanding RM 4,861.11. Chong's: principal repaid RM 1,388.89, interest RM 437.50, outstanding RM 48,611.11. The fee taken (RM 7.09 and RM 70.88) and the net credited (RM 175.55 and RM 1,755.51) are facts of this payout and live on its allocation, not on the holding.
  • RepaymentSchedule unchanged. The plan is the promise.
Equity today
Same shape for the holder. The equity holder record keeps a running cost basis and realised gain per holder, updated by each sale or dividend event, exactly as the register row keeps its running repayments. Different trigger: a company pays dividends when it declares them, not on a schedule. Both products will share the same payout tables.
ticket: F4.2 collection and F4.3 distribution · figures are illustrative; F4.1 defines the maths
Stage 6 · Service · only if things change
10

Chong negotiates an early exit; later a payment is missed; finally the note is repaid

Admin

Three things can happen after the loan is live, and each touches a different row. If Chong negotiates an early buyback of his 500 units, his register row alone records it; the note carries on, with its outstanding principal reduced if the issuer was the buyer. If an instalment is not received by its due date, the platform counts the days past due from the schedule row and moves the note to Late; at 90 days for a monthly note it becomes Default in recovery, and Admin's recovery process takes over. If Admin agrees a restructure, a third schedule version is added and the earlier promise stays on file. When the last instalment is paid, the note moves to Closed. The register rows stay forever as the holders' record and the issuer's track record.

We record
  • NoteRegister Chong's row: holding status Active → Exited, with the exit date and amount, and his principal outstanding set to zero. The other 188 rows are untouched.
  • Note status Performing → Late → Default in recovery, or Restructured, and finally Closed, with the closing date. On a restructure the note's rate, tenure and maturity change with the new schedule version; the offer on CampaignNoteTerm stays as it was disclosed.
  • RepaymentSchedule version 3, "Restructured", only if a Supplemental Deed is signed.
Equity today
Same shape. A holder's status on the counter changes when that holder sells; the counter's own status changes when the company's listing changes. Different content: shares never fall behind or close, so the note's status set is its own.
ticket: lane G servicing writes the note's states · buyback and refund are holding transitions on the register · days past due are computed, never stored on the plan
If you already know equityKeep four things in mind. A register row is one per holder per note, merged across that holder's investments exactly as a Share is; each investment points at its row, as Investment.ShareId does. Minting is one step once Admin confirms success, with no draft to review and nothing to scale back. The note's status lives on the note's row and each holder's status on the register row; both are moved only by the events above, never from a status screen. And there is no secondary market for notes; nothing in these tables blocks one later.

So, in one paragraph, this ticket buildsThree shared columns (one of them, AssetClass, renamed from ProductType), one renamed column (price per share becomes price per asset) and a slug rule on Campaign, with a migration that backfills existing campaigns as equity, Islamic where the share class or payment account says so. Instrument-agnostic names and meanings for Investment's quantity, price and asset-type columns, plus a link from each paid investment to its holder's register row, with lots unused for notes. Five new tables: the note's terms as offered, the note itself with the terms and servicing terms in force, its status and running totals, the register with one row per holder carrying that holder's status and running share of repayments, and the schedule in versions with its rows. The running totals are written only by payouts and always reconcile to them. Investment increments, for equity and notes alike, are a separate ticket and out of scope here. Four operations that other tickets call: create or update terms, mint the note and its register at close, mark the note performing at disbursement, and add a schedule version. A raise that is refused after close never gets a note; its refunds are on the investments. Two seeded notes to prove it, and three admin read endpoints to look at them. Nothing customer-facing, no payments, no configuration values, and no status screen.
